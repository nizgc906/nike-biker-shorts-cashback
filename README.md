## Start with the inseam, because that decides everything else

Inseams are listed in inches on Nike's US store and in centimetres almost everywhere else, which is why the same product shows up as "3"" and "7.5cm" in different tabs. They're identical.

- **3" / 7.5cm** — barely a biker short. Gym and lifting. You'll wear these under a loose tee.
- **5" / 12.5cm** — the middle ground most people want for training, spin and errands.
- **7" / 18cm** — full coverage, no thigh gap drama. Running, cycling, walking around.
- **8" / 20cm and up** — Universa territory, with pockets. Closest to a compression short.

If you've had a pair ride up mid-run, the inseam is usually the culprit, not the fabric. Shorter inseams ride up more on thicker thighs. Nike Pro Sculpt reviewers on Nike's own product page say exactly that: "the only one thing I would change is adding something to keep the thigh from rolling up when running."

## Sizing: assume you might need to go up

## See-through is a real question, and there's a real answer

This is the thing people search before buying, so: the thinner Dri-FIT One and Pro fabrics are the ones where light colours get scrutinised. The Universa line is explicitly built around it — "moisture-concealing technology" and squat-proof stretch — and the Pro Seamless drops the front seam so the fabric doesn't ride.

In practice, buy black or a dark tonal colour if you plan to squat or cycle in daylight, and save pale colourways for the pairs you wear to coffee. The NikeSKIMS Matte pair, which is the palest and most sculpting of the lot, has buyers confirming the opacity holds up: "not transparent. Used them for both running and pilates."

## The choice that matters more than brand

High-rise versus mid-rise is the decision people skip, then regret.

High-rise sits at or above the natural waist and pairs with cropped tops without a gap. It also holds more of the core in, which is why the Sculpt and Universa pairs feel "locked in." Mid-rise sits just below the natural waist and gives you more freedom through the ribcage — better if you hate pressure across the stomach during cardio.

The catch with high-rise is the waistband. If it's a wide jacquard elastic, it resists rolling. If it's soft and thin, it folds over the moment you hinge. Read the waistband description, not the waistband height.
